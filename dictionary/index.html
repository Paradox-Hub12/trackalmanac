F1 dictionary

386 Formula 1 terms, from aerodynamics and tyres to flags, radio codes and fan slang.

#

107% rule
A driver slower than 107% of the fastest Q1 time may not start the race unless the stewards allow it. (Race weekend & results)

A

ABS also: Anti-lock brakes
Anti-lock brakes, which stop wheels locking under braking. Banned since 1994. (Power unit & electronics)
Accident data recorder also: ADR
A 'black box' that records crash forces, compulsory since 1997. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Active aero also: Active aerodynamics
From 2026, the front and rear wings move between two settings during a lap: corner mode (wings closed, full downforce) and straight mode (wings opened on designated straights to cut drag). It is available to every driver on every lap, unlike DRS. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Active suspension
Computer-controlled suspension that kept the car at the ideal height. Williams dominated with it in 1992–93; banned for 1994. (Power unit & electronics)
Aero rake also: Rake sensor
A scaffold-like metal grid of pressure sensors (pitot tubes) bolted to a car in testing or practice to measure the airflow behind the wheels, wings or floor. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Aerodynamic device
Any part shaped to change airflow. The 2009 and 2022 rules removed many of them to make following easier. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Aerodynamics also: Aero
How air flows over and under the car. In F1 it is about creating downforce for grip while keeping drag low. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Aggregate qualifying
A 2005 format adding together times from two sessions. Dropped mid-season. (History & eras)
Air intake also: Airbox
The opening above the driver's head that feeds air to the engine (and in older cars a tall airbox). (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Allocation
The fixed number of parts or tyres each driver may use, such as power units or tyre sets. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Apex also: Hitting the apex, Clipping point
The innermost point a driver aims for in a corner. (Racecraft & strategy)
Appeal
Challenging a stewards' decision at the FIA court of appeal. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Aquaplaning
When a tyre rides on a layer of standing water and loses contact with the road. (Tyres & handling)
Armco also: Guardrail
Steel crash barrier rails. (Rules, flags & penalties)
ATR also: Aerodynamic Testing Restrictions, Sliding scale
Aerodynamic Testing Restrictions: since 2021 teams get wind-tunnel and CFD time on a sliding scale, with the champion team getting the least. (Aerodynamics & chassis)

B

Back of the grid also: Pit lane start
A penalty that puts a driver at the rear of the grid. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Backmarker also: Lapped car, Lapped traffic
A slower car at the back of the field, often about to be lapped. (Racecraft & strategy)
Balance
How evenly grip is shared between front and rear. Drivers adjust it with front-wing angle and settings on the steering wheel. (Tyres & handling)
Ballast
Weights added to bring a car up to the minimum weight and adjust its balance. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Bargeboards also: Turning vanes
Complex vanes between the front wheels and the sidepods that steered the turbulent wake of the front tyres away from the floor. Effectively banned by the 2022 rules. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Beam wing
A small lower wing element below the main rear wing that works with the diffuser to pull air out from under the car. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Bite
Grip and response from the front end on turn-in. (Tyres & handling)
Black and orange flag also: Meatball flag
A car has dangerous damage and must pit to have it fixed. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Black and white flag
A warning for unsporting behaviour or repeated track-limits offences, like a yellow card. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Black flag
The driver is disqualified and must return to the pits. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Blistering
When the inside of a tyre overheats so much that rubber bubbles up and breaks away from the surface. (Tyres & handling)
Blown diffuser also: Exhaust-blown diffuser
Using exhaust gas blown through the diffuser to add downforce, even off the throttle by keeping the engine firing. Peaked with Red Bull in 2011; restricted from 2012. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Blue flag
Shown to a car about to be lapped: let the faster car through. Ignoring three in a row brings a penalty. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Bodywork also: Body
The car's outer carbon panels: engine cover, sidepods, nose and so on. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Bono, my tyres are gone
Lewis Hamilton to his race engineer Peter Bonnington, often just before setting fast laps. (Radio & commentary)
Boost also: Boost button
A 2026 button the driver can press anywhere on the lap to deploy maximum electrical power, as long as there is energy in the battery. (Power unit & electronics)
Boost pressure also: Boost (turbo)
How hard the turbo pushes air into the engine, in bar. Limited to 4.0 bar in 1987 and 2.5 bar in 1988 before turbos were banned. (Power unit & electronics)
Bottoming out also: Bottoming
When the underside of the car (the plank and its titanium skid blocks) strikes the track, usually at high speed or over bumps, throwing sparks. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Box, box also: Box box box, Box this lap
The radio call telling a driver to pit this lap. From German Boxenstopp, pit stop. (Radio & commentary)
Brake bias also: Brake balance
How braking force is split between front and rear axles. Drivers adjust it corner by corner from the steering wheel. (Power unit & electronics)
Brake magic
Mercedes' name for a steering-wheel setting (2020) that shifted brake bias sharply forward to heat the front tyres behind the Safety Car. (Power unit & electronics)
Brake-by-wire also: BBW
The electronic rear brake system that blends normal hydraulic braking with the braking effect of the MGU-K harvesting energy. (Power unit & electronics)
Braking point
The spot where a driver starts braking for a corner. (Racecraft & strategy)
Bwoah
Kimi Räikkönen's catchphrase. (Fan slang & stories)

C

Camber
The tilt of the tyres seen from the front. F1 cars run negative camber (tops leaning in) for cornering grip. Track camber is the slope of the road surface itself. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Carbon fibre also: Carbon composite
Strong, light woven carbon material set in resin, used for almost the whole car since McLaren's MP4/1 of 1981. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Catch fencing
Wire fences once used to slow cars that left the track. Banned in 1986 because they caused injuries. (Rules, flags & penalties)
CFD also: Computational Fluid Dynamics
Computational Fluid Dynamics: simulating airflow on computers. Limited by the rules alongside wind-tunnel time. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Chassis
The main structure of the car, built around the monocoque, to which engine and suspension are attached. 'Constructor' means the team that builds it. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Chequered flag also: Checkered flag
The black-and-white flag that ends a race or session. (Race weekend & results)
Chicane
A tight left-right or right-left pair of corners that slows the cars down. (Racecraft & strategy)
Classified
A driver who completes at least 90% of the winner's distance counts as a finisher, even if he stopped. (Race weekend & results)
Clean air
Undisturbed air in front of a car with nobody close ahead. A car in clean air has its full downforce and cooling. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Clipping also: Derating
When the battery stops deploying before the end of a straight, so the top speed flattens out or drops. (Power unit & electronics)
Coandă effect also: Coanda exhaust
The tendency of a gas jet to follow a curved surface. Around 2012–2013 teams used it to bend hot exhaust gases down toward the diffuser for extra downforce. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Cockpit
The space where the driver sits. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Cold tyres
Tyres below their working temperature, with little grip, common on out-laps and restarts. (Tyres & handling)
Commercial petrol also: Pump fuel, Avgas
From 1958 cars had to run on fuel close to aviation petrol instead of alcohol mixtures. Fuel today must be close to road fuel, and sustainable from 2026. (History & eras)
Compounds also: C1–C6, Tyre compounds
Pirelli's dry slick tyres run from C1 (hardest) to C6 (softest, added in 2025). Three are chosen for each race and marked Hard (white), Medium (yellow) and Soft (red). (Tyres & handling)
Concorde Agreement
The contract between teams, the FIA and Formula One Management covering prize money and governance. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Constructor
A team that builds its own chassis. Since 1981 every team must be a constructor. (Team & paddock)
Constructors' Championship
The title for the team with the most points from both cars. (Race weekend & results)
Contact patch
The small area of each tyre actually touching the road at any moment. (Tyres & handling)
Control Electronics also: CE
The power unit's electronic brain, managing energy flow between battery, motor and engine. (Power unit & electronics)
Cool-down lap also: Slowing-down lap
The slow lap after the chequered flag or a fast run. (Racecraft & strategy)
Cooldown room
The room where the top three wait before going to the podium. (Team & paddock)
Copy also: Understood
Radio word for message received. (Radio & commentary)
Corner mode also: Z-mode
The default high-downforce active-aero setting from 2026, with the wings closed for cornering grip. Early drafts of the rules called it Z-mode. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Cost cap also: Budget cap, Financial regulations
The yearly limit on what a team may spend on car performance. US$135 million (base) for 2023–2025; US$215 million from 2026, when more costs came under it. Driver salaries are excluded. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Cosworth DFV also: DFV
The 3-litre V8 that Ford funded and Cosworth built in 1967. Cheap and widely available, it won 155 races into the 1980s. (Power unit & electronics)
Countback
How ties in the championship are broken: most wins, then most second places, and so on. (Race weekend & results)
Crash test also: Impact test
Impact tests every chassis must pass before racing: front from 1985, side and rear later. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Crashgate
The 2008 Singapore GP scandal: Renault ordered Nelson Piquet Jr. to crash to bring out a Safety Car that helped Fernando Alonso win. (Fan slang & stories)
Curfew
Hours at night when team staff may not work on the cars, with a few exceptions per season. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Customer team
A team that buys its power unit, and sometimes other parts, from another team. (Team & paddock)
CVT also: Continuously variable transmission
Continuously variable transmission: a gearbox with no fixed gears, tested by Williams in 1993 and banned before it raced. (Power unit & electronics)
Cylinders also: V6, V8 engine, V10 engine, V12
The chambers where fuel burns. V6, V8, V10 and V12 mean six, eight, ten or twelve cylinders arranged in a V shape. (Power unit & electronics)

D

Debrief
Meetings after sessions where drivers and engineers go over the data. (Team & paddock)
Defending also: Covering the inside
Protecting a position by taking the inside line into corners. (Racecraft & strategy)
Degradation also: Deg
Loss of tyre performance over a stint, from heat as well as wear. A tyre can lose grip even with plenty of tread left. (Tyres & handling)
Delta also: Delta time
A time difference, such as between laps or between two cars. Under a VSC drivers must stay above a minimum delta time. (Racecraft & strategy)
Deploy also: Deployment
To use stored electrical energy for extra power. (Power unit & electronics)
Development driver
A driver who works on the simulator and in tests to help develop the car. (Team & paddock)
Differential also: Diff
The device that lets the rear wheels turn at different speeds through corners. Drivers tune it for corner entry, mid-corner and exit. (Power unit & electronics)
Diffuser
The upswept rear section of the floor. It lets the fast air from under the car slow and expand, which keeps pressure under the car low and sucks it onto the track. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Dirty air also: Turbulent air, Wake
The hot, turbulent wake behind a car. A driver following closely loses downforce, slides more and overheats tyres and brakes, which makes overtaking harder. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Divebomb also: Send it, Late lunge
A very late-braking lunge down the inside from a long way back. (Racecraft & strategy)
DNF / DNS / DSQ also: DNF, DNS, DSQ
Did Not Finish, Did Not Start, Disqualified. (Race weekend & results)
DNQ also: Did not qualify, Pre-qualifying
Did Not Qualify: too slow to make the grid, common when more cars entered than could start. (Race weekend & results)
Do the opposite
A strategy call: if the car ahead pits, stay out; if it stays out, pit. (Racecraft & strategy)
Double diffuser
A loophole of 2009 in which Brawn, Toyota and Williams built a second diffuser deck for extra downforce. Banned from 2011. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Double points
In 2014 only, the final race awarded double points. (History & eras)
Double stack also: Double-stacking
Pitting both team cars on the same lap, one behind the other. (Racecraft & strategy)
Downforce
Aerodynamic load pushing the car down onto the track. More downforce means more grip and faster cornering, at the cost of more drag. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Drag
Aerodynamic resistance slowing the car down, most noticeable on straights. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Drift
A controlled slide with the rear tyres sliding. (Racecraft & strategy)
Drive to Survive also: DTS
Netflix's F1 documentary series (from 2019), credited with a big rise in new fans. (Fan slang & stories)
Drive-through also: Drive-through penalty
A penalty where the driver must drive through the pit lane at the speed limit. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Driver of the Day also: DOTD
A fan vote after each race. (Race weekend & results)
Drivers' briefing
The meeting before the race where the Race Director goes over procedures with drivers. (Team & paddock)
Drivers' Championship also: World Champion
The world title for the driver with the most points over the season. (Race weekend & results)
Drivers' parade
A lap of the circuit before the race when drivers wave to fans. (Team & paddock)
Dropped scores also: Best results count
Until 1990 only a driver's best results counted toward the title, so some points were dropped. (History & eras)
DRS also: Drag Reduction System
Drag Reduction System, used from 2011 to 2025: an opening flap in the rear wing (up to 85 mm) that a driver within one second of the car ahead at a detection point could use in designated zones. Replaced by active aero and overtake mode in 2026. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
DRS train also: Trulli train
A queue of cars each within a second of the one ahead, so all have DRS and nobody can pass. (Racecraft & strategy)
DRS zone
A designated straight where DRS could be opened, with a detection point before it (2011–2025). (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Dual-axis steering also: DAS
Mercedes' 2020 system that changed the front wheels' toe when the driver pushed or pulled the steering wheel. Banned from 2021. (Aerodynamics & chassis)

E

El Plan also: The Plan
A fan meme built around Fernando Alonso's 'master plan'. (Radio & commentary)
Endplate
The vertical panel at each end of a wing, managing the vortices that form where high- and low-pressure air meet. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Energy Store also: ES, Battery
The lithium-ion battery that holds harvested electrical energy. (Power unit & electronics)
Engine braking
The slowing effect of the engine and energy recovery when lifting off and changing down, which also helps rotate the car into a corner. (Power unit & electronics)
Engine capacity also: Displacement, cc, Cubic capacity
The total volume of the engine's cylinders, in litres or cc (cubic centimetres). 1.6 litres = 1600 cc. A bigger capacity usually means more power. (Power unit & electronics)
Engine freeze also: Development freeze
A rule stopping makers from developing their engines, used from 2007 and in the hybrid era, to cut costs. (Power unit & electronics)
Engine mode also: Strat mode, Engine map, Party mode
A power-unit setting chosen from the steering wheel, from fuel-saving to maximum attack. Teams use code names on the radio. (Power unit & electronics)
ERS also: Energy Recovery System
Energy Recovery System: the hybrid part of the power unit that harvests energy, stores it in the battery and deploys it as extra power. (Power unit & electronics)
Esses also: S-bends
A sequence of quick left-right-left corners, like the S in their name. (Racecraft & strategy)

F

F-duct
A 2010 McLaren idea: the driver covered a hole in the cockpit to send air to the rear wing and stall it on straights. Banned from 2011, when DRS took over. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Fan car
The Brabham BT46B of 1978, which used a large fan to suck air from under the car. It won its only race, in Sweden, and was withdrawn. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Fastest lap
The quickest lap of the race. It earned a bonus point (if in the top ten) from 2019 to 2024. (Race weekend & results)
FIA
Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile, the body that governs F1 and writes its rules. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Five-second penalty also: Time penalty, 10-second penalty
Time added at the next pit stop (before work starts) or to the race time. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Flat bottom also: Flat floor
A flat floor between the wheels, required from 1983 to 2021 to limit ground-effect downforce. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Flat spot also: Flatspot
A flat patch ground into a tyre when a wheel locks under braking. It makes the car vibrate badly. (Tyres & handling)
Floor also: Underfloor
The underside of the car. Since 2022 it produces most of the downforce through shaped Venturi tunnels. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Flow-vis also: Flow visualisation paint
Fluorescent paint (usually paraffin-based) sprayed on the bodywork in practice. Air streaks the paint as it dries, showing engineers how the flow moves over the car. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
FOM also: Formula One Management, Liberty Media
Formula One Management, the commercial rights holder, owned by Liberty Media. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Formation lap also: Warm-up lap, Parade lap
The lap before the start, behind the pole sitter, to warm tyres and brakes. (Race weekend & results)
Formula Two also: F2
The junior single-seater class. In 1952 and 1953 the World Championship was run to its rules (2-litre engines) to get more cars on the grid. (History & eras)
Free pit stop also: Free stop
A gap to the car behind big enough to pit without losing a place, roughly the time a stop costs (about 20–25 seconds at most tracks). (Racecraft & strategy)
Free practice also: FP1, FP2, FP3, Practice
Practice sessions on Friday (and Saturday at non-sprint weekends) with no points at stake: FP1, FP2 and FP3. (Race weekend & results)
FRIC
Front-and-rear interconnected suspension, which kept the car level under braking and cornering. Banned in 2014. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Front row
The first two places on the grid. (Race weekend & results)
Front wing
The wing at the nose. It creates front downforce and shapes the airflow for the rest of the car. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Fuel cell also: Fuel bladder, Fuel tank
The flexible, puncture-resistant fuel tank inside the monocoque. (Power unit & electronics)
Full wets also: Wets, Extreme wets
Blue-marked tyres for heavy rain, clearing about 85 litres of water a second per tyre at speed. (Tyres & handling)

G

G-force also: g
The force drivers feel in braking, cornering and acceleration, up to about 5–6 g in F1. (Racecraft & strategy)
Gap / interval
Gap is the time to the leader; interval is the time to the car directly ahead. (Racecraft & strategy)
Garage
The team's working space in the pit building. (Team & paddock)
Gearbox also: Seamless shift
An eight-speed seamless-shift gearbox, changed with paddles behind the steering wheel in milliseconds. (Power unit & electronics)
Gentleman driver
An amateur who raced on private money, common in early F1. (Fan slang & stories)
Gloves
Fireproof gloves, with sensors that send the driver's pulse and blood oxygen to the medical team. (Rules, flags & penalties)
GP2 engine
Fernando Alonso's 2015 Japanese GP complaint about the Honda power unit. (Radio & commentary)
Graining
Small rolls of rubber that tear off a tyre sliding on a cool surface and stick back onto the tread, cutting grip until they wear away. (Tyres & handling)
Grand Chelem also: Grand Slam
Pole, win, fastest lap and leading every lap. Jim Clark holds the record with eight. (Race weekend & results)
Grand Prix also: GP
French for 'great prize': the name for each championship race. (Race weekend & results)
Grandes Épreuves also: Grande Épreuve
The most important Grands Prix of the 1920s–40s. Before 1950 there was no championship; these races carried the prestige. (History & eras)
Gravel trap
A bed of gravel beyond a corner to slow a car that goes off. (Racecraft & strategy)
Green flag
Track clear, or the start of a session. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Green track
A track with little rubber laid down, such as on Friday morning or after rain, which feels slippery. (Tyres & handling)
Grid also: Starting grid
The starting positions in two staggered lines. (Race weekend & results)
Grid penalty
A driver starting a set number of places back, often for using extra power-unit parts. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Grid walk
The broadcast walk among the cars on the grid before the start, made famous by Martin Brundle. (Team & paddock)
Grip also: Mechanical grip, Aero grip
How well the tyres hold the road. It comes from the tyres themselves (mechanical grip) and from downforce (aerodynamic grip). (Tyres & handling)
Grooved tyres
Dry tyres with four grooves (three on the front in 1998), used from 1998 to 2008 to cut grip and speeds. (Tyres & handling)
Ground effect
Producing downforce mainly from the underside of the car, with shaped tunnels creating low pressure beneath it. First used in F1 by Lotus in 1977–78, banned in 1983 and brought back in 2022. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Gurney flap
A small vertical lip on the trailing edge of a wing that adds a lot of downforce for little drag. Named after Dan Gurney. (Aerodynamics & chassis)

H

Hairpin
A very slow, tight 180-degree corner. (Racecraft & strategy)
Halo
The titanium cockpit protection hoop above the driver's head, compulsory since 2018. It must withstand about 125 kN, roughly 12 tonnes. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Hammer time also: Hammertime
Mercedes' call to Lewis Hamilton to push as hard as possible. (Radio & commentary)
Handling
How a car behaves through corners: balance, response and stability. (Tyres & handling)
HANS device also: HANS
Head and Neck Support: a carbon collar linked to the helmet that stops violent head movement in crashes. Compulsory since 2003. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Hard / Medium / Soft also: White, yellow, red
The three dry compounds nominated for a race, with white, yellow and red sidewall markings. Softer means more grip and faster wear. (Tyres & handling)
Harvest also: Harvesting
To recover energy into the battery, mainly under braking. (Power unit & electronics)
Hat-trick
Pole position, race win and fastest lap at the same Grand Prix. (Race weekend & results)
He turned into me also: He pushed me off
A common driver radio complaint meant for the stewards. (Radio & commentary)
Headrest
The padded cockpit sides that protect the driver's head. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Heat cycle
One warm-up and cool-down of a tyre. Each cycle changes the rubber slightly. (Tyres & handling)
Helmet
Compulsory since 1952; today's carbon helmets pass strict FIA tests. (Rules, flags & penalties)
High wings
Tall wings mounted on stalks directly to the suspension in 1968–69. Several broke and caused crashes, so they were banned mid-1969. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Homologation
Official FIA approval that freezes a design, such as a power unit or crash structure, for a set period. (Power unit & electronics)
Honey Badger
Daniel Ricciardo's nickname. (Fan slang & stories)
Hybrid era also: Turbo hybrid
From 2014: turbo hybrid power units with energy recovery. (History & eras)

I

ICE also: Internal combustion engine
Internal combustion engine: the 1.6-litre turbocharged V6 at the heart of the power unit. (Power unit & electronics)
Iceman
Kimi Räikkönen's nickname. (Fan slang & stories)
In-lap
The lap ending with a pit stop. (Racecraft & strategy)
Indianapolis 500
Part of the World Championship from 1950 to 1960, though few F1 regulars took part. (History & eras)
Inerter also: J-damper
A suspension part (also called a J-damper) that helped control tyre bounce. Banned from 2022. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Installation lap also: Installation run
A short lap at the start of a session to check that the car works. (Race weekend & results)
Intermediates also: Inters
Green-marked treaded tyres for a damp track or light rain. At speed each tyre clears about 30 litres of water a second. (Tyres & handling)

J

Jack man also: Front jack, Rear jack
The mechanic who lifts the front or rear of the car. (Team & paddock)
Jump start
Moving before the lights go out, which brings a penalty. (Race weekend & results)
Junior programme also: Driver academy
A team's academy for young drivers on their way to F1. (Team & paddock)

K

Kerbs also: Sausage kerbs
Painted, ridged strips on the edges of corners. Taller sausage kerbs stop drivers cutting corners. (Racecraft & strategy)
KERS also: Kinetic Energy Recovery System
Kinetic Energy Recovery System: the first hybrid boost (2009, then 2011–2013), worth about 80 hp for under seven seconds a lap. (Power unit & electronics)

L

Lap record
The fastest race lap ever set on a circuit's current layout. (Racecraft & strategy)
Lapped
A car the leader has passed a full lap ahead of. (Racecraft & strategy)
Launch control
Electronic systems that managed wheelspin at the start. Banned; drivers now control the clutch themselves with a paddle. (Power unit & electronics)
Laying down rubber
Cars leaving rubber on the racing line, which adds grip over a session. (Tyres & handling)
Leave me alone, I know what I'm doing
Kimi Räikkönen to his engineer on the way to winning the 2012 Abu Dhabi GP. (Radio & commentary)
Leaving a car's width
Giving a rival who is alongside enough room. Not doing so can bring a penalty. (Racecraft & strategy)
Left-foot braking
Braking with the left foot while the right stays on the throttle; standard in F1 since cars lost clutch pedals. (Racecraft & strategy)
Licking the stamp and sending it
Daniel Ricciardo's phrase for committing to a late-braking pass. (Racecraft & strategy)
Lift and coast also: LiCo
Lifting off the throttle before the normal braking point and rolling into the corner to save fuel, cool the brakes or engine, or harvest energy. (Power unit & electronics)
Lights out also: Lights out and away we go
The start of a race, when the five red lights go out. (Race weekend & results)
Lights out and away we go
David Croft's signature start call on Sky Sports F1. (Radio & commentary)
Lock-up also: Locking up
When a wheel stops turning under braking and slides, often with blue smoke from the tyre. (Tyres & handling)
Lollipop also: Lollipop man
The sign once used to tell a driver to go; now mostly replaced by lights. (Team & paddock)
Long run
A practice run of many laps on heavy fuel to test race pace and tyre wear. (Racecraft & strategy)

M

Marbles
Small balls of discarded rubber that collect off the racing line. Running over them feels like driving on ice. (Tyres & handling)
Marshal also: Marshals
A trackside volunteer who waves flags, clears debris and helps after crashes. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Mass damper
A spring-mounted weight inside the nose used by Renault in 2005–06 to steady the car over kerbs. Banned mid-2006. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Media pen
The area where drivers give interviews after sessions. (Team & paddock)
Medical car
A fast car with a doctor that follows the first lap and responds to crashes. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Medical centre
The trackside hospital every circuit must have. (Rules, flags & penalties)
MGU-H also: Motor Generator Unit – Heat
Motor Generator Unit–Heat: a generator on the turbocharger that harvested energy from exhaust heat and cut turbo lag. Used 2014–2025, dropped for 2026. (Power unit & electronics)
MGU-K also: Motor Generator Unit – Kinetic
Motor Generator Unit–Kinetic: an electric motor on the drivetrain that harvests energy under braking and gives it back as power. Up to 120 kW until 2025; 350 kW from 2026. (Power unit & electronics)
Minimum weight
The lowest weight a car (with driver) may be. 800 kg until 2025, 768 kg from 2026. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Minister of Defence
Fan nickname for Sergio Pérez after his defence against Hamilton at the 2021 Abu Dhabi GP. (Fan slang & stories)
Monocoque also: Survival cell, Tub
The carbon-fibre survival cell that holds the driver and fuel tank, built to stay intact in heavy impacts. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Motorhome also: Hospitality
The team's temporary building in the paddock for meals, meetings and media. (Team & paddock)
Moving under braking
Changing line in the braking zone, which is dangerous and can be penalised. (Racecraft & strategy)
Mr Saturday
Fan nickname for George Russell's qualifying speed. (Fan slang & stories)
Multi 21
Red Bull's 2013 Malaysian GP code for 'car 2 ahead of car 1': Webber ahead of Vettel. Vettel ignored it and won. (Radio & commentary)
My tyres are gone also: Tyres are shot
The tyres have no grip left. (Radio & commentary)

N

Naturally aspirated also: Atmospheric engine, Normally aspirated
An engine that breathes air at normal pressure, without a turbo or supercharger. (Power unit & electronics)
Net position also: Net lead
Where a driver will be once everyone has made the same number of pit stops. (Racecraft & strategy)
Net Zero 2030
F1's target to be carbon neutral by 2030. (Fan slang & stories)
Night race
A race held after dark under floodlights, first at Singapore in 2008. (Race weekend & results)
Nose also: Nose cone
The front of the chassis that carries the front wing and contains a crash structure tested in impact tests. (Aerodynamics & chassis)

O

Off-camber also: Banking
A corner where the track slopes away from the inside, pushing cars outward. Positive camber or banking does the opposite. (Racecraft & strategy)
One move rule
A defending driver may make one change of direction to defend a position. (Racecraft & strategy)
One-stop / two-stop also: One-stopper, Two-stopper
How many pit stops a strategy plans. (Racecraft & strategy)
Opposite lock also: Countersteer
Steering against a slide to catch an oversteering car. (Tyres & handling)
Option / prime tyre also: Option tyre, Prime tyre
Older names (2007–2010s) for the softer (option) and harder (prime) of the two dry compounds at a race. (Tyres & handling)
Orange army
Max Verstappen's Dutch fans, in orange. (Fan slang & stories)
Out of the window also: Operating window, Tyre window
A tyre outside its working temperature range: too cold to grip, or too hot and degrading. (Tyres & handling)
Out-braking also: Late braking
Braking later than the driver alongside to take the corner first. (Racecraft & strategy)
Out-lap
The lap from the pit lane, used to warm tyres before a flying lap or race stint. (Racecraft & strategy)
Outwash also: Inwash
Bodywork designed to push the front-tyre wake outward, away from the floor. The 2022 rules were written to reduce it; inwash directs air inward instead. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Overalls also: Race suit, Nomex
Fireproof race suits made of Nomex, tested to resist fire for several seconds. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Overcut
Staying out longer than a rival who has pitted, using clear air to stay ahead after your own stop. (Racecraft & strategy)
Oversteer also: Loose, Snap
The rear tyres run out of grip first, so the back of the car slides. (Tyres & handling)
Overtake mode also: MOM, Manual Override Mode
From 2026: a driver within one second of the car ahead at the detection point gets extra electrical energy (+0.5 MJ) to use on the next lap, and can keep full 350 kW deployment up to 337 km/h while other cars taper off from 290 km/h. Called Manual Override Mode (MOM) in early drafts. (Power unit & electronics)

P

P1, P2… also: Position
Finishing or running positions: P1 is first place. (Race weekend & results)
Pace also: Race pace, Quali pace
A car's speed. Quali pace is one-lap speed; race pace is speed over a long run. (Racecraft & strategy)
Paddles also: Paddle shift
The levers behind the steering wheel for changing gear and working the clutch. (Team & paddock)
Paddock
The private area behind the garages with team motorhomes, hospitality and offices. (Team & paddock)
Paddock Club
The premium hospitality suites above the garages. (Team & paddock)
Paddock pass
The pass that gets you into the paddock. (Fan slang & stories)
Papaya rules
McLaren's guidelines for letting its drivers race each other fairly, from 2024. (Fan slang & stories)
Parc fermé
From qualifying until the race, most of the car's setup cannot be changed without starting from the pit lane. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Party mode also: Quali mode
Fan name for the maximum-power qualifying engine modes of the late 2010s. From late 2020 teams had to use the same mode in qualifying and the race. (Power unit & electronics)
Pay driver
A driver whose seat depends largely on money brought from sponsors or family. (Team & paddock)
Penalty points
Points on a driver's superlicence for driving offences. Twelve within 12 months brings a one-race ban. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Picking up rubber
Driving over marbles on the slowing-down lap to collect rubber, sometimes to help the car meet the minimum weight. (Tyres & handling)
Pit board
The sign held over the pit wall with gap and lap information, before radio became standard. (Team & paddock)
Pit crew also: Pit stop crew
About 20 mechanics who change tyres in a stop: wheel-gun operators, tyre on and off, front and rear jacks. (Team & paddock)
Pit lane also: Pits
The lane alongside the garages where pit stops happen. (Team & paddock)
Pit lane speed limit also: Pit limiter
Usually 80 km/h in the pit lane, enforced by a limiter button. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Pit loss
The time a pit stop costs compared with staying out. (Racecraft & strategy)
Pit stop
Stopping in the pit box to change tyres or fix damage. The record is 1.80 seconds by McLaren (Norris, Qatar 2023). (Team & paddock)
Pit wall
The covered stand on the pit straight where the team principal, strategists and engineers watch the race. (Team & paddock)
Pit window
The range of laps when a planned stop makes most sense. (Racecraft & strategy)
Plan A / B / C also: Plan B, We are checking
Pre-planned race strategies. Ferrari's 'Plan E' and 'We are checking' became fan jokes. (Radio & commentary)
Plank also: Skid block, Skid plank
A 10 mm wood-composite board under the car that enforces a minimum ride height. If it is worn by more than 1 mm the car is disqualified. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Podium
The top three finishers, and the stage where they receive trophies. (Race weekend & results)
Points also: Points system
25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1 for the top ten in a Grand Prix. A point for fastest lap was given from 2019 to 2024. (Race weekend & results)
Pole position also: Pole, Pole sitter
First place on the starting grid, earned by the fastest qualifier. (Race weekend & results)
Porpoising also: Bouncing
Violent bouncing at speed on ground-effect cars: the floor gets so close to the track that the airflow stalls, the car pops up, regains downforce and is sucked down again. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Power unit also: PU, Engine
The complete F1 engine. In 2014–2025: a 1.6-litre V6 turbo (ICE) plus turbocharger, MGU-K, MGU-H, battery and control electronics. From 2026 the MGU-H is gone and the electric share is close to half of total power. (Power unit & electronics)
Power unit allocation also: Engine penalty
Each driver may use only a set number of each power-unit component per season. Using more triggers grid penalties. (Power unit & electronics)
Pre-season testing also: Testing
The few days of official testing before the first race. (Race weekend & results)
Protest
A formal complaint by a team about another team or a decision. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Push lap also: Flying lap, Hot lap
A lap driven flat out. (Racecraft & strategy)
Push now also: Push push, Empty the battery
Go flat out this lap. (Radio & commentary)
Push-rod / pull-rod also: Pushrod, Pullrod
Two ways of linking the wheels to the springs and dampers inside the car. Which one is used affects packaging and airflow. (Aerodynamics & chassis)

Q

Q1 / Q2 / Q3 also: Q1, Q2, Q3
The three knockout parts of qualifying, used since 2006. The slowest cars drop out of Q1 and Q2 (six each with 22 cars from 2026), and the top ten fight for pole in Q3. (Race weekend & results)
Qualifying also: Quali
The session that sets the grid, in three knockout parts: Q1, Q2 and Q3. (Race weekend & results)

R

Race Director also: Race Control
The FIA official who runs the race: starts, flags, Safety Cars and red flags. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Race engineer also: Race engie
The engineer who speaks to the driver on the radio and runs the car's setup. (Team & paddock)
Racing incident
A collision the stewards decide was nobody's fault, with no penalty. (Racecraft & strategy)
Racing line
The fastest path around a circuit. (Racecraft & strategy)
Rake
The nose-down, tail-up stance of a car. High-rake cars (Red Bull before 2022) turned the whole floor into a bigger diffuser. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Rear wing
The wing at the back that produces rear downforce. Its flap opened for DRS until 2025 and moves with active aero from 2026. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Rear-engined also: Mid-engined
A car with the engine behind the driver. Cooper's success in 1959–60 made it the standard layout. (Power unit & electronics)
Recharge
The energy a car may harvest per lap under the 2026 rules (braking, lifting, part-throttle and super clipping). The allowance varies by circuit. (Power unit & electronics)
Reconnaissance lap also: Recon lap
A lap from the pit lane to the grid before the race, used to check conditions. (Race weekend & results)
Red and yellow striped flag also: Slippery surface flag
Slippery surface ahead: oil, water or debris. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Red flag
Session or race stopped. All cars return slowly to the pit lane. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Refuelling
Topping up fuel during pit stops, allowed 1994–2009 and banned since 2010. (History & eras)
Reprimand
A formal warning. Five in a season (at least four for driving) bring a grid penalty. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Reserve driver also: Third driver
A driver ready to step in if a race driver cannot race. (Team & paddock)
Retirement also: Retired, Ret
Stopping during a race because of damage, a fault or a crash. (Race weekend & results)
Rev limit also: rpm, Rev limiter
The maximum engine speed allowed: 19,000 rpm in 2007, 18,000 rpm 2009–2013, and 15,000 rpm in the hybrid era. (Power unit & electronics)
Ride height
The distance between the floor and the track. Lower usually means more downforce, until the car starts bottoming or porpoising. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Right of review
A team can ask the stewards to look again at a decision when there is significant new evidence. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Roll hoop also: Roll bar, Roll structure
The strong hoop above the driver's head that protects him if the car flips. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Rolling start
A start while moving, behind the Safety Car, used in very wet conditions. (Race weekend & results)
Rookie
A driver in his first F1 season. Teams must give FP1 sessions to rookies twice a year per car. (Race weekend & results)
Run-off also: Run-off area
The area beyond the track edge, made of gravel, grass or asphalt. (Racecraft & strategy)

S

Safety Car also: SC
A road car that leads the field at reduced speed after an incident. Overtaking is banned. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Safety Car line
A painted line after which the Safety Car rules end and racing resumes. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Sandbagging
Hiding a car's real speed in testing or practice with heavy fuel or low engine modes. (Race weekend & results)
Scrubbed tyres also: Scrubs
Tyres that have been through one short heat cycle, such as an out-lap and in-lap, so they are no longer new. (Tyres & handling)
Scrutineering
Technical checks by the FIA to make sure cars meet the rules. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Scuderia
Italian for 'stable', the word in Ferrari's team name. (Team & paddock)
Sector also: Sector times
Each lap is split into three timed sectors. (Racecraft & strategy)
Sending it also: Full send, Send it
Committing fully to a risky move. (Racecraft & strategy)
Shakedown also: Filming day
A short first run of a new car to check it works. Filming days are limited to 200 km on special tyres. (Race weekend & results)
Shark fin also: Engine cover fin
A vertical fin on the engine cover to steady airflow to the rear wing, used in various years. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Shoey
Drinking champagne from a racing boot on the podium, made famous in F1 by Daniel Ricciardo. (Fan slang & stories)
Sidepods
The bodywork on each side of the cockpit, holding the radiators and side-impact structures and shaping air toward the rear. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Silly season
The months of driver-market rumours and seat changes. (Team & paddock)
Simply lovely
Max Verstappen's signature celebration on the radio. (Radio & commentary)
Simulator also: Sim, Driver-in-the-loop
A full-size driving rig at the factory used to prepare setups and learn tracks. (Team & paddock)
Single-lap qualifying also: One-lap qualifying
2003–2005: each driver ran one timed lap alone on track. (History & eras)
Six-wheeler also: Tyrrell P34
The Tyrrell P34 of 1976–77, with four small front wheels. It won the 1976 Swedish GP. (History & eras)
Size zero
Packaging the engine and gearbox as tightly as possible for slimmer bodywork. Best known from McLaren-Honda's 2015 design. (Power unit & electronics)
Slicks
Dry tyres with no tread pattern, for maximum rubber on the road. Used since 1971, apart from the grooved-tyre years 1998–2008. (Tyres & handling)
Sliding skirts
Skirts on ground-effect cars that slid down to seal the floor to the track. Banned in 1981. (History & eras)
Slipstream also: Tow, Draft
Running close behind another car on a straight, where the car ahead has punched a hole in the air. The follower has less drag and gains speed. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Smooth operator
Carlos Sainz's song on the radio after good results. (Radio & commentary)
Snap oversteer also: Snap, Tankslapper
A sudden, violent loss of rear grip that needs instant opposite lock to save. (Tyres & handling)
Sole supplier also: Single supplier
A single maker supplies every team, as with tyres since 2007 and the standard ECU. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Speed trap
A timing point on the fastest straight that records top speed. (Racecraft & strategy)
Sporting director
The team manager who deals with rules, the FIA and race procedures. (Team & paddock)
Sporting regulations
The rules about how races and weekends are run. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Sprint also: Sprint race
A short Saturday race of about 100 km with points for the top eight (8 down to 1), held at six weekends a season. (Race weekend & results)
Sprint Qualifying also: Sprint Shootout
The short qualifying session that sets the sprint grid. (Race weekend & results)
Spygate
The 2007 scandal in which McLaren held Ferrari technical data. McLaren was fined US$100 million and lost its constructors' points. (Fan slang & stories)
Squeeze also: Pinching
Pushing a rival toward the edge of the track to make them back off. (Racecraft & strategy)
Stall also: Stalled
When the engine cuts out, most often at the start. (Racecraft & strategy)
Standard ECU also: ECU
The FIA-standard electronic control unit every team must use, made by McLaren Applied, which polices driver aids. (Power unit & electronics)
Standing start
The usual F1 start from a stationary grid. (Race weekend & results)
Stay out
Cancel the pit stop and stay on track. (Radio & commentary)
Stewards
The panel of officials, including an ex-driver, who judge incidents and give penalties. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Sticker tyres also: New tyres, Stickers
Brand-new tyres, still with the manufacturer's stickers on. (Tyres & handling)
Stint
The laps run on one set of tyres. (Racecraft & strategy)
Stop-go also: Stop-and-go
A penalty where the driver stops in his box for 10 seconds with no work done. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Straight mode also: X-mode
The low-drag active-aero setting used from 2026: front and rear wing flaps open on designated straights to raise top speed. Early drafts of the rules called it X-mode. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Super clipping also: Superclipping
A 2026 technique: harvesting energy at the end of a straight while still flat on the throttle, so the car's speed tops out early. (Power unit & electronics)
Supercharger also: Compressor, Supercharged
A compressor driven by the engine itself that forces more air in for more power. Allowed in early F1 (also called a compressor). (Power unit & electronics)
Superlicence also: Super licence
The licence needed to race in F1, earned with points from junior series. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Suspension
The springs, dampers and wishbones joining the wheels to the car, set up to control grip and ride height. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Sustainable fuel also: Advanced sustainable fuel
From 2026 F1 cars run on fully sustainable fuel, made from non-fossil sources such as waste or captured carbon. (Power unit & electronics)
Switchback also: Cutback, Over-under
Letting a rival go into a corner first, then turning back underneath them for a better exit. (Racecraft & strategy)

T

T-wing
A small wing mounted on top of the engine cover in 2017, banned for 2018. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Team orders also: Swap positions
Instructions from the team for one driver to let the other past or hold position. Legal again since 2011. (Racecraft & strategy)
Team principal also: TP, Team boss
The person in charge of a team at races and overall. (Team & paddock)
Technical directive also: TD
An FIA note clarifying how the technical rules should be read. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Technical director also: TD (technical director)
The engineer in charge of designing the car. (Team & paddock)
Technical regulations
The rules about how cars must be built. (Rules, flags & penalties)
TecPro also: TecPro barrier
Linked foam-filled barrier blocks that absorb impacts better than tyre walls. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Telemetry also: Data
The live stream of data from hundreds of sensors on the car to engineers in the garage and at the factory. (Power unit & electronics)
Temple of Speed
Nickname for Monza, the fastest circuit on the calendar. (Fan slang & stories)
The cliff also: Falling off a cliff, Off the cliff
The moment a worn tyre suddenly loses its remaining grip, and lap times drop by seconds. (Tyres & handling)
Tifosi
Ferrari's passionate fans. Italian for 'fans', from tifo, a word linked to typhus fever. (Fan slang & stories)
Toe also: Toe-in, Toe-out
The angle of the wheels seen from above: toe-in points them toward each other, toe-out away. It affects stability and turn-in. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Token system also: Engine tokens
From 2014 to 2017, a limited number of 'tokens' each power-unit maker could spend on upgrades. (Power unit & electronics)
Torque
The twisting force an engine delivers, which drives acceleration out of corners. (Power unit & electronics)
Track evolution also: Rubbering in
The track getting faster as rubber is laid down and dust blown away during a session or weekend. (Tyres & handling)
Track limits also: White line
The white lines at the edge of the track. Going beyond them with all four wheels can lose a lap time or bring a penalty. (Racecraft & strategy)
Track position
Being ahead on the road, which matters most where overtaking is hard. (Racecraft & strategy)
Track temperature also: Track temp
The temperature of the asphalt, which strongly affects tyre grip and wear. (Tyres & handling)
Track walk
Drivers and engineers walking the circuit on Thursday to study it. (Team & paddock)
Traction
The grip the rear tyres have under acceleration. (Tyres & handling)
Traction control also: TC
Electronics that cut power to stop wheelspin. Banned since 2008, when a standard ECU was introduced. (Power unit & electronics)
Tractor
Slang for a slow, uncompetitive car. (Fan slang & stories)
Trulli train
A queue of faster cars stuck behind a slower one, after Jarno Trulli, who often held up the field this way. (Racecraft & strategy)
Turbo era
1977–1988, when turbocharged engines produced over 1,000 hp in qualifying. Turbos were banned from 1989 and returned in 2014. (History & eras)
Turbocharger also: Turbo
A compressor driven by exhaust gas that forces extra air into the engine. Used 1977–1988 and again since 2014. (Power unit & electronics)
Two into one doesn't go
A commentators' line for two cars trying to take the same piece of track and colliding. (Racecraft & strategy)
Two-compound rule also: Mandatory pit stop
In a dry race each driver must use at least two different dry compounds. It does not apply if wet or intermediate tyres are used. (Tyres & handling)
Tyre barrier also: Tyre wall
Stacks of tyres bolted together in front of walls to soften impacts. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Tyre blankets also: Tyre warmers
Electric covers that heat tyres before they go on the car. Still allowed, at lower temperatures than in the past. (Tyres & handling)
Tyre offset
The difference in tyre age or compound between two cars. (Racecraft & strategy)
Tyre pressures
Pirelli sets minimum starting pressures for each race, which the FIA checks. (Tyres & handling)
Tyre war
Periods when two tyre makers competed in F1, notably Bridgestone and Michelin from 2001 to 2006. Single supplier since 2007, Pirelli since 2011. (History & eras)

U

Undercut
Pitting before the car ahead to use the speed of new tyres and be in front when they stop. (Racecraft & strategy)
Understeer also: Push, Washout, Pushing
The front tyres run out of grip first, so the car will not turn enough and runs wide. (Tyres & handling)
Unlapping also: Unlap
Lapped cars overtaking the leaders behind the Safety Car to rejoin the lead lap before a restart. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Unsafe release
Sending a car out of its pit box into another car's path. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Upgrade also: Upgrade package
A package of new parts (floor, wing, sidepods) brought to a race to make the car faster. (Aerodynamics & chassis)

V

V10 / V8 eras also: V10, V8
Naturally aspirated V10s ran from 1995 to 2005, then 2.4-litre V8s until 2013. (History & eras)
Venturi tunnels
The shaped channels under the floor of the post-2022 cars that speed up the air and generate ground-effect downforce. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Virtual Safety Car also: VSC
Every driver slows to a set delta time without a physical Safety Car, which keeps the gaps roughly the same. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Visor also: Tear-off strip, Tear-offs
The clear front of the helmet. A Zylon strip has reinforced its top edge since 2011. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Vortex also: Y250 vortex
A spinning tube of air created on purpose by wing tips or floor edges, used to seal the low pressure under the car. The Y250 vortex came off the middle of the pre-2022 front wing. (Aerodynamics & chassis)

W

Wear also: Tyre wear
Physical loss of rubber from the tread. (Tyres & handling)
Weaving
Zig-zagging on the straight to break a slipstream or block. Only one defensive move is allowed. (Racecraft & strategy)
Wheel gun
The air gun that removes and fits the single wheel nut. (Team & paddock)
Wheel-to-wheel
Two cars racing side by side. (Racecraft & strategy)
Wheelbase
The distance between the front and rear axles. Limited to 3.6 m until 2025 and 3.4 m from 2026. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
White flag
Slow vehicle ahead, such as a recovery truck or a damaged car. (Rules, flags & penalties)
Wind tunnel
A test facility that blows air over a scale model (up to 60%) to measure downforce and drag. Time in it is limited by the rules. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Works team also: Factory team
A team run by a car or engine maker. (Team & paddock)
World Motor Sport Council also: WMSC
The FIA body that approves rule changes. (Rules, flags & penalties)

Y

Yellow flag also: Double yellow
Danger ahead: slow down, no overtaking. Double waved yellows mean be ready to stop. (Rules, flags & penalties)

Z

Zero keel
A front-suspension layout of the 2000s in which the lower wishbones met under a raised nose with no keel beneath it, freeing airflow under the car. (Aerodynamics & chassis)
Zylon
A super-strong fibre used in helmet visor strips, tethers and anti-intrusion panels in the cockpit sides. (Rules, flags & penalties)
