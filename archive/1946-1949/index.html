Before the World Championship

Grand Prix racing 1946–1949

Grand Prix racing restarted in 1946, but the Formula 1 World Championship only began in 1950. From 1946 to 1949 there were no points and no champion: every race stood on its own. The most prestigious races were named Grandes Épreuves by the FIA.

  • The new Formula A, soon called Formula One, allowed 1.5-litre supercharged or 4.5-litre unsupercharged engines. It became the official formula on 1 January 1947, though some races still used other rules.
  • Alfa Romeo, Maserati, Talbot-Lago, ERA and later Ferrari built most of the winning cars.
  • The first World Championship round was the British Grand Prix at Silverstone on 13 May 1950.

Grandes Épreuves

The most prestigious races, 1947–1949

In motorsport history, Grandes Épreuves (French for “great trials” or “major events”) was a term used from 1906 to 1949 to designate the most prestigious and important international motor racing events. There were none in 1946.

1946

18 races

The first season after the Second World War. There was no championship and no race counted as a Grande Épreuve. Raymond Sommer was the most successful driver with five wins (one shared), and Maserati won half of the races.

  • The Turin Grand Prix in September was the first race run to the new Formula A (Formula One) rules.
  • Alfa Romeo won six races, including Turin and Milan, and the Grand Prix des Nations in Geneva.

1947

33 races

The first full season of Formula One rules. The Swiss, Belgian, Italian and French Grands Prix were the Grandes Épreuves. Alfa Romeo won three of them and 13 races in all; Luigi Villoresi won six races, more than anyone else.

  • Jean-Pierre Wimille won the Swiss and Belgian Grands Prix for Alfa Romeo.
  • Louis Chiron won the French Grand Prix at Lyon-Parilly for Talbot-Lago.
  • The season began in South America, with races in Brazil and Argentina.

1948

23 races

Maserati won 13 of the 23 races here, and Luigi Villoresi was the top winner again with six. The four Grandes Épreuves were Monaco, Switzerland, France and Italy: Wimille won two for Alfa Romeo, Farina won Monaco for Maserati and Trossi won in Switzerland.

  • Achille Varzi was killed in practice for the Swiss Grand Prix at Bremgarten.
  • Silverstone held its first British Grand Prix, won by Villoresi.
  • Nino Farina won the Circuito del Garda in a Ferrari.

1949

26 races

The last year before the World Championship. Alfa Romeo stayed away, and Maserati, Ferrari and Talbot-Lago shared the wins. Five races were Grandes Épreuves. Alberto Ascari and Juan Manuel Fangio won five races each.

  • Jean-Pierre Wimille was killed in practice in Buenos Aires in January.
  • Fangio's first European season brought wins at San Remo, Pau, Perpignan, Marseille and Albi.
  • Ascari won the Swiss and Italian Grands Prix for Ferrari.
